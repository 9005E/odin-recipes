Dal Tadka

Description

Dal Tadka is a quintessential North Indian lentil dish renowned for its comforting, hearty flavors, primarily made from split yellow lentils such as toor dal (pigeon peas) or a combination of lentils like masoor and moong, which are pressure-cooked or simmered until they achieve a creamy, smooth consistency. The signature element is the "tadka" or tempering, where ghee or oil is heated with aromatic spices including cumin seeds, mustard seeds, garlic, ginger, dried red chilies, asafoetida, and sometimes onions or tomatoes, releasing their essential oils and infusing the dal with a burst of savory, spicy depth before being poured over the cooked lentils. Often garnished with fresh cilantro and a squeeze of lemon for brightness, this versatile vegetarian staple is commonly served alongside steamed rice, naan, or roti, making it a staple in both home-cooked meals and restaurant menus across India and beyond.

Ingredients

  • Dal (lentils): Any kind of lentils can be used.
  • Aromatics: Onions, fresh ginger and garlic are used for flavour and texture.
  • Tomatoes: While fresh ripe tomatoes are preferred, you can use peeled or diced canned tomatoes.
  • chilies: You may use fresh green chilies like Indian or Thai chilies for heat and flavour.
  • Ground spices: Turmeric, garam masala and red chili powder are essential for this recipe.
  • Kasuri methi: Dried fenugreek leaves, which impart North Indian flavours to your dal.
  • Lemon juice: Used for serving.

For the Tadka

  • Ghee: Can be substituted for high somke point oils like coconut, avocado, peanut oil.
  • Whole spices: Mustard, cumin, dried red chilies are the 3 ingredients you can use.
  • Hing: Also known as asafoetida, a key ingredient of the tadka.

Steps

  1. Add 1 cup of your dried lentils to a pot.
  2. Rinse the lentils well, rubbing with your fingers. The water should become clear.
  3. Add 3 cups of fresh water to the pot. bring the water to a rolling boil on a high heat, then reduce the heat to medium and cook uncovered until letils are soft.
  4. While the dal cooks, heat 2 tablespoons oil and add 1/2 teaspoon cumin seeds and fry lightly until they begin to splutter.
  5. Add 1 tablespoon fine chopped garlic and ginger to the oil. Fry until aromatic.
  6. Add 1/2 cup fine chopped onions and 1 green chilli to the oil, Fry until the onions are transulcent, stirring from time to time.
  7. Reduce the heat. Add 1/4 teaspoon turmeric, 3/4 teaspoon red chilli powder and 1/2 teaspoon garam masala. Stir through well.
  8. Add 1 cup deseeded and fine chopped tomatoes. Add 1 teaspoon salt.
  9. Saute until tomatoes are softened.
  10. Meanwhile, check if the dal is done cooking. You want the texture of the dal to be soft cooked and mushy.
  11. At this stage, you may add additional boiling water to the dal to adjust the consistency, as required.
  12. When the onion and tomato mixture is ready, add it to the cooked dal and mix.
  13. Simmer for 6 to 7 minutes. note that dal will become thickend upon cooling, so adjust consistency as needed. taste test and add salt if required.
  14. Crush 1 tablespoon kasuri methi between your palms andd add it to the boiling dal. Also add and mix in the coriander leaves. Turn off the heat.
  15. In a small pan, melt 2 tablespoons ghee (or oil). Add 3/4 teaspoon cumin seeds, 1/4 teaspoon mustard seeds (optional), and saute over low heat until spices crackle.
  16. Mix in 2 whole dried red chilies.
  17. Add 1 tablespoon fine chopped garlic and fry lightly until aromatic.
  18. Mix in 1/8 teaspoon asafoetida and 1/3 teaspoon red chilli powder. Immediately remove from heat.
  19. Pour the hot tadka over the dal, Garnish dal tadka with coriander leaves before serving.
  20. Serve in a bowl with basmati rice and rotti or naan, as preferred.
